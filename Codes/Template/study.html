About the Study
Understand how sleep habits influence academic success.
Research Background
Students’ sleeping habits are often affected by academic pressure, deadlines, and technology use. Many students sacrifice their recommended 7–9 hours of sleep to complete schoolwork and meet academic demands. Although this may help them keep up with their studies, consistently getting less sleep can lead to unhealthy sleep patterns and poorer sleep quality.
Research Objectives
  1. To develop a web-based platform that visually displays students' sleep habits and academic performance data, enabling users to easily track, interpret, and understand patterns in their sleep behavior over time.
  2. To analyze and present the relationship between sleep patterns and academic performance through the platform, using clear, accessible visual representations (e.g., charts, trends, summaries) that help students recognize how their sleeping habits may be influencing their academic outcomes.
  3. To raise student awareness of healthy sleep practices by providing evidence-based information and personalized data insights through the platform, encouraging greater engagement in monitoring and improving their own sleep habits.
Research Gap
Most studies on student sleep focus on identifying problems rather than providing practical solutions. Research among Filipino college students found that students may get sufficient sleep duration but still experience poor sleep quality (Estacio et al., 2025). This study aims to bridge that gap by developing a web system that visually presents students’ sleep data and provides personalized recommendations, such as reducing late-night phone use and avoiding staying up late.
Importance of the Study
This study is important because it helps students understand how their sleeping habits may affect their thoughts, emotions, and academic performance. The proposed website provides an easy-to-understand way for students to analyze their sleep habits and receive practical recommendations for improvement.

CONCLUSION

Sleep timing has a weak relationship with GPA

Based on the six sleep-timing variables, none showed a strong relationship with GPA. Correlations ranged from −0.02 to −0.21 across 254 students. Wake-up time showed the strongest relationship, with weekday wake time at r = −0.21 and weekend wake time at r = −0.17.

Overall, sleep timing alone does not appear to strongly affect academic performance. Study habits, course workload, stress, and personal circumstances may be more important factors.

💡
RECOMMENDATION

Maintain a consistent sleep schedule

Although changing sleep schedules may not directly improve GPA, students can still benefit from consistent sleep for their overall well-being.

Future research should examine sleep alongside stress, workload, and study habits. It should also use more accurate sleep tracking and include students from different institutions.
